## What it is

Natural graphite is mined as flake, vein or amorphous graphite. For batteries, flake concentrate is milled into spherical particles, purified to 99.95% carbon, traditionally with hydrofluoric acid, and coated. Synthetic graphite, made from petroleum coke at very high temperature, competes for the same anode market. The Critical Raw Materials Act lists battery-grade graphite, natural and synthetic, as strategic.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China mines most of the world's natural graphite, followed by Madagascar, Mozambique, Brazil and Tanzania. Its hold on processing is tighter still: it produces nearly all spherical graphite and the large majority of finished anode material, natural and synthetic. The EU has small mines in Austria and Germany, and large resources in Sweden, Finland and Norway under development, but imports nearly all of its needs. Since December 2023 China has required export licences for battery-grade graphite products, and it tightened these controls for the United States in late 2024.

## Substitution and recycling

Synthetic graphite substitutes directly and already holds the larger share of the anode market, but it is more energy intensive and depends on Chinese capacity just as much. Silicon is added to anodes in small proportions to raise capacity. Graphite is rarely recovered from spent batteries today, although recyclers are developing processes.

## What to watch

Chinese export licensing, anode plants under construction in Scandinavia and elsewhere in Europe, mines in Mozambique, Tanzania and Sweden, and the share of silicon in anodes.
