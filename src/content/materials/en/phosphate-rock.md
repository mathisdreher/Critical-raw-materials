## What it is

Phosphate rock is a sedimentary or igneous ore rich in the mineral apatite. It is treated with sulphuric acid to produce phosphoric acid, the intermediate for fertilisers such as DAP, MAP and TSP. A small share of acid is purified for food, feed and technical uses, including batteries.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China, Morocco, the United States and Russia are the main producers. Morocco, including Western Sahara, holds by far the largest reserves and is the leading exporter of rock, acid and fertilisers. The EU has one significant mine, in Finland, and imports most of its needs as rock, acid and finished fertilisers from Morocco, Russia, Algeria, Egypt and Israel. Fertiliser prices spiked in 2021 and 2022 when China restricted exports and the war in Ukraine disrupted Russian trade. EU tariffs on Russian fertilisers adopted in 2025 are progressively reducing that source. EU limits on cadmium in fertilisers also favour low-cadmium igneous rock.

## Substitution and recycling

Phosphorus cannot be substituted in agriculture, but it can be recycled: manure and sewage sludge already return phosphorus to soils, and several Member States now require recovery from sludge or its ash. Recovered struvite and ash-based products are recognised under the EU Fertilising Products Regulation.

## What to watch

Moroccan production and pricing, Chinese export restrictions, demand for purified acid from battery makers, and phosphorus recovery obligations in wastewater legislation.
