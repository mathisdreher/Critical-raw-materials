## What it is

Arsenic is rarely mined for itself. It is captured as arsenic trioxide from the flue dust of smelters that treat copper, gold and lead ores, partly because environmental rules require its removal. A small fraction is purified into arsenic metal, and a still smaller fraction into the 99.9999% grades that the semiconductor industry needs.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China, Peru and Morocco account for most arsenic trioxide output. High-purity metal is produced by a handful of companies, mainly in China, Japan and Germany. The EU produces arsenic compounds as a smelter by-product, notably in Belgium, but relies on imports for metal. Because arsenic is a by-product, its supply follows copper and gold smelting rather than its own demand, and tighter environmental inspections in producing countries can cut availability abruptly.

## Substitution and recycling

Use of arsenic in wood preservatives, pesticides and glass has been phased out or restricted in the EU for health reasons, so substitution has largely already happened where it was possible. In semiconductors, silicon-germanium and gallium nitride compete with gallium arsenide in some radio-frequency applications. Arsenic is not recycled from end-of-life products in meaningful quantities, although scrap from wafer manufacturing is reprocessed.

## What to watch

Demand for radio-frequency and photonic chips, the availability of high-purity refining capacity outside China, and EU chemicals regulation, where arsenic compounds are subject to authorisation.
