## What it is

Lithium is extracted from two types of deposit: hard-rock spodumene, mined mainly in Australia and increasingly in Africa, and brines pumped from salt flats in Chile and Argentina. Both are converted into battery-grade lithium carbonate or lithium hydroxide. The Critical Raw Materials Act designates battery-grade lithium as strategic.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Australia, Chile, China and Argentina provide most of mined supply, with Zimbabwe and Brazil growing. Refining is far more concentrated: China converts roughly two thirds of the world's lithium chemicals, including most Australian spodumene. The EU currently imports all the battery-grade lithium it uses, historically mostly carbonate from Chile, and a first refinery has opened in Germany. Mining projects in Portugal, Czechia, Finland, France, Germany and Serbia are at various stages, several with strategic project status. Batteries account for close to 90% of demand, so the lithium price follows electric vehicle and storage sales closely. After the 2022 peak, oversupply pushed prices down by more than 80%, leading to mine curtailments.

## Substitution and recycling

Sodium-ion batteries avoid lithium and are entering low-cost vehicles and stationary storage, but with lower energy density. Within lithium-ion batteries there is no substitute. Recycling recovers lithium from black mass, although less easily than nickel and cobalt. The EU Batteries Regulation sets a lithium recovery target of 50% by the end of 2027 and 80% by the end of 2031, and minimum recycled content from 2031.

## What to watch

Electric vehicle and storage demand, restarts of curtailed mines, direct lithium extraction projects, and the first European refineries and mines reaching production.
