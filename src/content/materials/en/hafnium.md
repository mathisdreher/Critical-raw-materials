## What it is

Hafnium always occurs with zirconium in the mineral zircon, at a ratio of roughly one to fifty. The two metals are chemically almost identical and are separated only when zirconium is purified for nuclear fuel cladding, which must be hafnium-free. Hafnium supply is therefore tied to the nuclear zirconium industry, not to its own demand, and world output is below a hundred tonnes per year.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

France and the United States produce most of the world's hafnium, with smaller volumes from China and Russia. This makes hafnium one of the rare materials on the list for which the EU is a net exporter at the processing stage, even though the zircon feedstock is imported from Australia, South Africa and elsewhere. Supply risk comes from the very small number of producers and from the inelastic, by-product nature of supply. Strong aerospace demand pushed prices to record highs in 2023.

## Substitution and recycling

Hafnium in superalloys is recycled together with the alloy scrap, but it is not recovered separately from end-of-life products. Zirconium can replace it in some superalloys, with lower performance. In nuclear control rods, silver-indium-cadmium alloys and boron carbide are alternatives.

## What to watch

Aircraft engine build rates, nuclear fuel demand, which sets zirconium and thus hafnium output, and capacity additions for electronic-grade hafnium.
