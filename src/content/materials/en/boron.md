## What it is

Boron is never found in elemental form. It is mined as borate minerals such as tincal, colemanite and ulexite, then refined into borax, boric acid and boron oxide. The Critical Raw Materials Act lists metallurgy-grade boron as strategic because of ferroboron, the alloy that carries boron into permanent magnets and special steels.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Türkiye holds most of the world's reserves and is by far the largest producer through its state-owned mining company. The United States, Chile, Bolivia, Peru and China supply the remainder. The EU has no borate mining and sources almost all of its imports from Türkiye, one of the highest single-country dependencies of the whole list. The relationship has been stable, but there is little alternative capacity if it were disrupted.

## Substitution and recycling

Borates are consumed in glass, ceramics and fertilisers and cannot be recovered. Recycling of glass returns some boron to the loop, but the end-of-life recycling input rate is around 1%. Substitutes exist only for individual uses, and none for boron in agriculture or in neodymium magnets.

## What to watch

Turkish production and export policy, new borate and lithium-borate projects in Serbia and the Americas, and demand growth from magnets and insulation.
