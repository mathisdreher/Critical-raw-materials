## What it is

Fluorspar is the commercial name for the mineral fluorite, calcium fluoride. It is sold in two main grades: acid grade, above 97% calcium fluoride, for the chemical industry, and metallurgical grade for steelmaking. Reacting acid-grade fluorspar with sulphuric acid yields hydrofluoric acid, the gateway to all fluorochemicals.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China produces more than half of the world's fluorspar but has become a net importer of the acid grade as its battery and fluorochemical industries expand. Mexico, Mongolia, South Africa and Vietnam are the other main suppliers. The EU has mines in Spain, Germany and Bulgaria that cover part of its needs and imports the rest, principally from Mexico and South Africa. Tightening Chinese mine safety inspections and growing Chinese demand have kept the market firm.

## Substitution and recycling

There is no substitute for fluorspar as the primary source of fluorine, apart from fluorosilicic acid recovered from phosphate fertiliser plants, which supplies part of the aluminium fluoride market. Recycling is limited to refrigerant recovery and some reuse of hydrofluoric acid in industry.

## What to watch

Chinese import demand, the EU regulation on fluorinated gases and the proposed restriction of PFAS, battery electrolyte capacity in Europe, and new mines in Canada, South Africa and Mongolia.
