## What it is

Silicon metal is produced by reducing quartz with carbon in submerged-arc furnaces, which consume around 11 to 13 megawatt-hours of electricity per tonne. The product is about 99% pure. It is distinct from ferrosilicon, an iron alloy used in steelmaking, and from polysilicon, the ultra-pure material obtained from silicon metal through the Siemens process.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China dominates production, in Xinjiang, Yunnan and Sichuan, followed by Brazil, Norway, the United States and France. The EU has smelters in France, Germany and Spain, and neighbouring Norway and Iceland are major suppliers, so import reliance at this stage is moderate. Downstream it is a different picture: China makes over 90% of the world's solar-grade polysilicon and wafers, and European solar manufacturing has contracted sharply. EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese silicon have been in place since 1990. Imports of products linked to Xinjiang face scrutiny under the EU forced labour regulation, which applies from late 2027.

## Substitution and recycling

There is no substitute for silicon in chips, silicones or mainstream solar cells. Recycling of silicon from end-of-life modules is at an early stage and recovers mostly glass and aluminium today, although the first dedicated plants have started operating in Europe.

## What to watch

European electricity prices, which have led to furnace curtailments, Chinese overcapacity in polysilicon, trade defence measures, and solar module recycling under the WEEE rules.
