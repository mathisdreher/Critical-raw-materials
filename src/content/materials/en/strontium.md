## What it is

Strontium is extracted from celestine, a strontium sulphate mineral, and converted into strontium carbonate, the main traded compound, and strontium nitrate. Its largest historical market, the glass of cathode-ray tube televisions, disappeared in the 2000s, and the industry is now much smaller.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Mining is concentrated in Iran, Spain, China and Mexico. Spain, with deposits near Granada, covers EU needs for celestine, so the EU has no import reliance at the mining stage. The supply risk score comes from the very small number of producing countries and companies, and from conversion to carbonate, where China and Mexico are the main producers. Because the market is small and volumes are low, trade data are volatile from year to year.

## Substitution and recycling

Barium can replace strontium in ferrite magnets with somewhat lower performance, and other colourants or LEDs can replace it in signalling. Strontium is not recycled: its uses are dissipative or locked into magnets that are not recovered.

## What to watch

Output of the Spanish mines, Iranian exports, demand for ferrite magnets as a rare-earth-free option, and the health of the European zinc smelting industry.
