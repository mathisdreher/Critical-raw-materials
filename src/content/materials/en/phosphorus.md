## What it is

Elemental phosphorus, traded as white or yellow phosphorus and written P4, is produced by reducing phosphate rock with coke in an electric arc furnace at around 1,500 °C. Only a few percent of mined phosphate takes this energy-intensive route. It is listed separately from phosphate rock because its supply chain, producers and uses are entirely different.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Production takes place in just four countries: China, by a wide margin, then Kazakhstan, Vietnam and the United States, which does not export. The last EU producer, in the Netherlands, closed in 2012. The EU therefore imports all its elemental phosphorus, mainly from Kazakhstan and Vietnam, with China supplying mostly derivatives. Output in China depends on hydropower availability in Yunnan and other south-western provinces, and power rationing there has repeatedly cut supply and raised prices. White phosphorus is also hazardous to ship, which limits trade flexibility.

## Substitution and recycling

For many derivatives there is no alternative to the furnace route, because purified wet-process phosphoric acid cannot supply the reduced phosphorus compounds needed. Elemental phosphorus is not recycled from products. Processes to produce P4 from sewage sludge ash are at pilot stage in Europe.

## What to watch

Chinese energy policy and export measures, output in Kazakhstan and Vietnam, demand from electrolyte salt production, and European projects to recover phosphorus from ash.
