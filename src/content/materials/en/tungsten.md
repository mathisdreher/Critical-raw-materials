## What it is

Tungsten is mined as wolframite and scheelite and concentrated to about 65% tungsten trioxide. Concentrate is converted into ammonium paratungstate, the main traded intermediate, then into tungsten oxide, metal powder and tungsten carbide. Prices are quoted per metric ton unit of tungsten trioxide.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China mines the great majority of the world's tungsten and dominates processing, with Vietnam, Russia, Bolivia, Rwanda and Australia as secondary sources. The EU is better placed than for many critical materials: it has mines in Austria, Portugal and Spain, an integrated producer in Austria, and a strong carbide and recycling industry in Germany, Sweden and Finland. It still imports concentrates and intermediates. In February 2025 China placed tungsten products under export licensing, and prices of ammonium paratungstate reached record levels during the year.

## Substitution and recycling

Tungsten is among the best recycled critical raw materials. Used carbide tools are collected and reprocessed chemically or by the zinc process, and scrap supplies over 40% of EU input. Substitution is limited: ceramics, cermets and coated tools replace tungsten carbide in some machining, and depleted uranium in some munitions.

## What to watch

Chinese export licensing and mining quotas, defence procurement, mine restarts in South Korea, Spain and the United Kingdom, and scrap availability.
