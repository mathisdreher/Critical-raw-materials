## What it is

Nickel comes from two types of ore. Sulphide ores, in Russia, Canada and Australia, are smelted into high-purity class 1 metal. Laterite ores, in Indonesia and the Philippines, were long used only for nickel pig iron and ferronickel for stainless steel. High-pressure acid leaching and matte conversion now turn laterites into battery-grade intermediates as well. The Critical Raw Materials Act lists battery-grade nickel as strategic. Nickel does not meet the supply-risk threshold of the criticality assessment.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Indonesia banned ore exports in 2020 to force processing at home, and with large Chinese investment it has raised its share of mined supply from about a quarter to well over half in five years. The resulting surplus pushed prices down and closed mines in Australia and New Caledonia. The EU mines and refines nickel in Finland, with Norway, Russia and Canada as traditional suppliers of class 1 metal. Russian nickel is not under EU sanctions, although the London Metal Exchange stopped accepting newly produced Russian metal in 2024.

## Substitution and recycling

Nickel is well recycled within stainless steel and alloy scrap. In batteries, lithium iron phosphate cells contain no nickel and have taken a large share of the market, which is the main substitution pressure. Battery recycling recovers nickel efficiently.

## What to watch

Indonesian ore quotas and royalties, the split between lithium iron phosphate and nickel-rich batteries, and the environmental footprint requirements of the EU Batteries Regulation.
