## What it is

The light rare earth elements are lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium, neodymium and samarium. They occur together in the minerals bastnaesite and monazite, and must be separated from each other by long solvent-extraction circuits before being reduced to metal and alloyed into magnets. Cerium and lanthanum are abundant and in surplus. Neodymium and praseodymium, often sold together as NdPr oxide, carry most of the value.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China mines the majority of the world's rare earths and performs an even larger share of separation, metal making and magnet production. The United States, Australia and Myanmar are the other significant miners, and the main separation plant outside China is in Malaysia. The EU has separation capacity in Estonia and France and a small magnet industry, but imports almost all of the magnets it uses, mostly from China. Deposits exist in Sweden, Norway and Greenland but none is in production.

## Substitution and recycling

Ferrite magnets and wound-rotor or induction motors can replace rare earth magnets in part of the market, with penalties in weight and efficiency. Recycling from end-of-life magnets is beginning but remains marginal. The Critical Raw Materials Act lists rare earths for permanent magnets as strategic and introduces labelling and recycled-content disclosure for magnets.

## What to watch

NdPr oxide prices, Chinese production quotas and export controls, the ramp-up of separation and magnet plants in Europe and North America, and price-floor or offtake support schemes from governments.
