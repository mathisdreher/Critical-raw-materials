## What it is

Manganese ore is mined in large open pits and underground operations, then smelted into ferroalloys for steel or processed chemically. Two refined products matter for technology: electrolytic manganese metal, and high-purity manganese sulphate monohydrate for batteries. The Critical Raw Materials Act lists battery-grade manganese as strategic.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

South Africa, Gabon and Australia supply most of the world's ore, and South Africa holds the largest reserves. China imports the bulk of traded ore and produces most of the world's ferroalloys, nearly all electrolytic manganese metal and over 90% of battery-grade sulphate. The EU mines little manganese and imports ore mainly from South Africa and Gabon for its ferroalloy plants in France, Spain and Slovakia. For battery-grade material it has almost no domestic capacity, although a project in Czechia based on old tailings and a refinery in Belgium aim to change that.

## Substitution and recycling

There is no substitute for manganese in steel. In batteries, lithium iron phosphate avoids it, while manganese-rich cathodes are being developed precisely to reduce nickel and cobalt. Manganese is recycled passively within steel scrap, and battery recycling recovers part of it.

## What to watch

Logistics in South Africa and Gabon, which regularly constrain ore exports, Chinese ferroalloy output, and the commissioning of battery-grade sulphate capacity outside China.
