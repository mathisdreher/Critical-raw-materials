## What it is

Gallium is not mined on its own. It occurs at trace levels in bauxite and zinc ores and is extracted from the liquor of alumina refineries. Only a small share of refineries are equipped to recover it. Crude gallium is then refined to the 99.9999% purity or more that semiconductor wafers require. World production is only a few hundred tonnes per year.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China built large recovery capacity during the 2010s, and low prices led producers in Germany, Hungary, Kazakhstan and the United Kingdom to stop primary production. As a result China now supplies almost all primary gallium. In August 2023 it introduced export licensing for gallium and germanium, and in December 2024 it banned exports to the United States. European buyers still receive material under licence, but volumes and prices have been unstable. Japan, South Korea and Russia have small primary output, and several restarts and new recovery units have been announced in Europe and elsewhere.

## Substitution and recycling

Scrap from wafer manufacturing is recycled intensively and provides a substantial secondary source. Gallium in end-of-life devices is too dispersed to recover. Silicon can replace gallium compounds only where lower performance is acceptable, and silicon carbide competes with gallium nitride in high-voltage power electronics.

## What to watch

Chinese export licensing, recovery projects at alumina refineries in Europe, and demand from gallium nitride power devices.
