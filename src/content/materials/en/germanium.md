## What it is

Germanium is a by-product. It is concentrated in certain zinc ores and in some coals, and recovered from zinc smelter residues or coal fly ash. It is traded as germanium dioxide, as metal, and as germanium tetrachloride for the fibre industry. Annual world supply is in the low hundreds of tonnes.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China produces the majority of refined germanium. Other sources are Russia, Canada, the United States and Belgium, where a refiner processes residues and recycled material. The EU therefore has processing capability but depends on imported feedstock. Since August 2023 Chinese exports require a licence, and shipments to the United States were banned in December 2024. Prices have multiplied since, and exports from China have fallen well below earlier levels. Recovery from zinc operations in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and North America is being developed as an alternative.

## Substitution and recycling

About a third of supply already comes from recycling, mostly manufacturing scrap from optics and fibre production. Zinc selenide and chalcogenide glasses can replace germanium in some infrared optics, and titanium or antimony catalysts are used for PET. In fibre and space solar cells there is no practical substitute.

## What to watch

Chinese export licence volumes, by-product recovery at zinc smelters outside China, satellite constellation demand, and defence procurement of infrared systems.
