## What it is

Niobium is mined from pyrochlore deposits and converted almost entirely into ferroniobium, an alloy containing about 65% niobium that steelmakers add in very small proportions. A minor share is refined into metal, oxides and alloys for aerospace, superconductors and electronics.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Brazil produces around nine tenths of the world's niobium, chiefly from one mine at Araxá, with a second Brazilian producer and one mine in Canada supplying the rest. The EU has no production and imports all its ferroniobium, mainly from Brazil. The dominant producer has kept capacity ahead of demand and prices stable for decades, so the high supply risk score reflects concentration rather than any history of disruption. Chinese companies hold minority stakes in Brazilian production and own the second producer.

## Substitution and recycling

Vanadium and titanium can replace niobium as microalloying elements, depending on relative prices, with some loss of performance. Niobium is recycled only within steel scrap, where it is diluted and not specifically recovered, so its functional recycling rate is close to zero.

## What to watch

Steel demand and the niobium intensity of steel in China and India, new projects in Africa and North America, and the emerging use of niobium oxides in battery anodes.
