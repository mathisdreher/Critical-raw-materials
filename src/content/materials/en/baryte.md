## What it is

Baryte (barite) is the mineral form of barium sulphate. Its value lies in a combination of high density, chemical inertness and low abrasiveness. Drilling-grade material is simply ground to specification. Chemical and filler grades require whiter, purer ore.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China and India produce more than half of world output, followed by Morocco, Kazakhstan and Iran. The EU has modest production, in Bulgaria and Germany among others, and imports the larger part of its needs, mainly from China and Morocco. Demand tracks drilling activity, which makes the market cyclical. Supply risk stems from the concentration of high-quality deposits and from transport costs, which are high relative to the value of the mineral.

## Substitution and recycling

Other weighting agents exist for drilling muds, such as hematite, ilmenite and celestite, but baryte remains preferred for technical and cost reasons. Drilling fluids are partly recovered and reused on site. Baryte in fillers and chemicals is not recycled.

## What to watch

Oil, gas and geothermal rig counts, Chinese and Indian export availability, freight costs, and specification changes that allow lower-density grades.
