## What it is

Scandium is fairly abundant in the Earth's crust but rarely concentrated. It is recovered as a by-product from titanium dioxide pigment waste, from rare earth and uranium processing, and from nickel laterite leaching. It is sold as scandium oxide or as an aluminium-scandium master alloy containing about 2% scandium. In the EU methodology it is assessed separately from the rare earth groups.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China is the principal producer, with Russia, the Philippines and, more recently, Canada contributing. The EU has no commercial production and imports all its needs. With global supply of a few tens of tonnes, the market is illiquid, prices are negotiated bilaterally, and potential users in aerospace and automotive have hesitated to design scandium into products without assured supply. In April 2025 China included scandium among the rare earths subject to export licensing. Recovery from bauxite residue has been piloted in Greece and elsewhere in Europe.

## Substitution and recycling

Yttria-stabilised zirconia replaces scandia in fuel cells at lower performance, and titanium or conventional high-strength aluminium alloys compete in aerospace. There is no recycling of scandium from end-of-life products.

## What to watch

New by-product supply from nickel, titanium and alumina operations in Australia, Canada and Europe, adoption of aluminium-scandium alloys by aircraft makers, and Chinese export licensing.
