## What it is

Titanium metal accounts for only a few percent of titanium mineral use. The rest becomes titanium dioxide pigment. Metal is made by the Kroll process: ilmenite or rutile is chlorinated to titanium tetrachloride, which is reduced with magnesium into a porous mass called sponge. Sponge is then melted, often several times, into ingots and forged into mill products. The 2023 list covers titanium metal specifically, and the regulation designates it as strategic.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Sponge is produced in China, Japan, Russia, Kazakhstan, Saudi Arabia, Ukraine and India. Only Japan, Russia, Kazakhstan and, increasingly, Saudi Arabia and China make grades qualified for rotating aerospace parts. The EU has no sponge production and imports sponge, ingots and scrap, although it has melting and forging capacity. A Russian producer was for years the largest supplier of titanium to European and American aircraft makers. It was left out of the EU sanctions adopted after the invasion of Ukraine, and manufacturers have been reducing their exposure since 2022 by qualifying other sources, a process that takes years.

## Substitution and recycling

Machining aerospace parts turns most of the ingot into chips, and this scrap is an important feedstock. Europe exports much of its titanium scrap, and new remelting plants in France aim to keep it in a closed loop. Substitutes such as steel, aluminium, nickel alloys or composites all carry weight or corrosion penalties.

## What to watch

Aircraft production rates, the status of Russian supply, new sponge and remelting capacity in Europe and Saudi Arabia, and electrolyser demand.
