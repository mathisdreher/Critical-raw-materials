## What it is

Magnesium metal is produced either by thermal reduction of dolomite with ferrosilicon, the Pidgeon process used in China, or by electrolysis of magnesium chloride from brines. The Pidgeon process is energy and carbon intensive and has historically run on coal. The strategic raw material in the regulation is magnesium metal, not the magnesite and magnesia used in refractories.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China accounts for the great majority of primary magnesium, concentrated in the provinces of Shaanxi and Shanxi. Smaller producers operate in the United States, Israel, Brazil, Russia, Kazakhstan and Türkiye. The EU closed its last primary smelter in 2001 after sustained low-priced imports and now depends on China for almost all supply. In autumn 2021, power rationing in China cut output sharply, prices tripled and European aluminium and automotive associations warned of imminent plant stoppages. Projects to restart primary production in Europe, including in Romania and Bosnia and Herzegovina, have been announced.

## Substitution and recycling

Magnesium is hard to replace as an alloying element in aluminium, where it has no equivalent. In castings, aluminium and engineering plastics are alternatives. Recycling is mostly indirect: magnesium is recovered within aluminium alloy scrap, and clean casting scrap is remelted.

## What to watch

Chinese energy and environmental policy in Shaanxi, European restart projects, and the carbon footprint of the Pidgeon process relative to electrolytic routes.
