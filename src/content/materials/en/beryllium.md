## What it is

Beryllium is extracted from bertrandite and beryl ores and processed into three families of products: copper-beryllium alloys containing up to about 2% beryllium, pure beryllium metal, and beryllium oxide ceramics. Inhaled beryllium dust is hazardous, so processing requires strict workplace controls.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

The supply chain is unusually concentrated. A single mine and plant in Utah, in the United States, provide most of the world's beryllium, with China and Kazakhstan as the other significant sources. The EU has no mining or primary processing and imports metal, master alloys and semi-finished products, mainly from the United States. The United States holds beryllium in its national defence stockpile, a sign of its strategic status.

## Substitution and recycling

Substitutes exist but usually at a loss of performance: titanium and aluminium composites in structures, phosphor bronze or copper-nickel-tin alloys in connectors. Manufacturing scrap is recycled efficiently by producers. Beryllium contained in end-of-life electronics is too diluted to recover.

## What to watch

Defence and space procurement, tightening occupational exposure limits in the EU, and progress of the few projects that could diversify supply beyond the United States.
