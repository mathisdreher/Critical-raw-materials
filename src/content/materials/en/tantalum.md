## What it is

Tantalum is recovered from tantalite and columbite-tantalite ores, known as coltan, and as a by-product of lithium and tin mining. Concentrates are chemically processed into potassium fluorotantalate and tantalum oxide, then reduced to capacitor-grade powder or metallurgical-grade metal.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

The Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda supply well over half of mined tantalum, much of it from artisanal and small-scale mining, followed by Brazil, Nigeria and China. Australia contributes as a by-product of lithium. Processing is done in China, Germany, the United States, Kazakhstan, Thailand and Japan. The EU imports all its primary tantalum, and its importers of ores and metal must carry out supply chain due diligence under the Conflict Minerals Regulation, in force since 2021. The conflict in eastern Congo, including the occupation of the Rubaya mining area in 2024, has repeatedly raised concerns about supply and traceability.

## Substitution and recycling

Ceramic and aluminium capacitors, and niobium capacitors, substitute for tantalum in many circuits, and have taken market share. Manufacturing scrap and spent sputtering targets are recycled and form a meaningful share of supply. Recovery from end-of-life electronics is negligible, because each device contains only milligrams.

## What to watch

Security and traceability in the Great Lakes region, by-product supply from lithium mines, and demand from data-centre and automotive electronics.
