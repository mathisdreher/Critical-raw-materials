## What it is

Cobalt is produced almost entirely as a by-product: of copper in the Central African Copperbelt and of nickel in Indonesia, Australia, Canada and elsewhere. It is shipped as hydroxide intermediate, then refined into metal for alloys or into sulphate and oxides for batteries.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

The Democratic Republic of the Congo mines well over half of the world's cobalt, and Indonesia has become the second source through its nickel laterite plants. China refines the majority of global output. The EU has refining capacity in Finland and Belgium, which makes it less dependent at the processing stage than for most battery materials, but the feedstock is imported. After a period of oversupply and very low prices, the Congolese government suspended exports in early 2025 and then replaced the ban with quotas, which lifted prices sharply. A small share of Congolese output comes from artisanal mining, which raises due-diligence obligations under EU battery rules.

## Substitution and recycling

Battery makers have steadily reduced cobalt content, and lithium iron phosphate cells use none. Cobalt is nevertheless hard to replace in superalloys and hard metals. It is one of the better recycled critical metals: alloys and catalysts are recovered, and battery recycling is scaling up, supported by the recovery targets of the EU Batteries Regulation.

## What to watch

Congolese export quotas, the pace of Indonesian supply growth, cathode chemistry choices by carmakers, and mandatory recycled-content levels for batteries.
