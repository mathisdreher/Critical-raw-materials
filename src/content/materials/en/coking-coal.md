## What it is

Coking coal is a category of bituminous coal with the caking properties needed to form strong coke when heated without air. Only part of the world's coal qualifies, and premium hard coking coal is concentrated in a few basins. It is distinct from thermal coal burned in power plants.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China is the largest producer and consumer. Seaborne trade is dominated by Australia, followed by the United States, Canada and, until sanctions, Russia. Within the EU, Poland and Czechia still mine coking coal, and the remainder is imported, principally from Australia and the United States. The EU embargo on Russian coal in 2022 forced steelmakers to reroute supply. Weather events in Queensland regularly disrupt the seaborne market and move prices.

## Substitution and recycling

Coke cannot be recycled, since it is consumed in the furnace. The structural substitute is a different steelmaking route: scrap melted in electric arc furnaces, or iron ore reduced with natural gas and increasingly hydrogen. European steelmakers have announced the replacement of several blast furnaces by direct reduction plants, which would cut coking coal demand over the next decade. The 2023 assessment kept coking coal on the list although it barely meets the threshold, and its status is regularly debated.

## What to watch

Blast furnace closures and direct-reduction projects in Europe, the EU Emissions Trading System and Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, and Australian export disruptions.
