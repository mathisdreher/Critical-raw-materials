## What it is

Copper is mined mostly from large, low-grade porphyry deposits, concentrated to around 30% copper, smelted and electro-refined into cathodes of 99.99% purity. The cathode is the traded commodity, priced daily on the London Metal Exchange, COMEX and the Shanghai Futures Exchange.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Mining is spread across Chile, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Peru, China, the United States and others, which is why the supply risk score is the lowest of the list. Smelting and refining are more concentrated: China processes well over 40% of the world's copper and continues to add capacity, squeezing treatment charges for smelters elsewhere. The EU mines copper in Poland, Spain, Sweden, Bulgaria and Portugal and has a strong smelting and recycling industry in Germany, Belgium, Poland and Spain, but imports roughly half of the concentrates it needs.

## Substitution and recycling

Copper is recycled without loss of quality, and scrap provides around half of EU supply. Aluminium substitutes in overhead lines and increasingly in cables and vehicle wiring when the price gap widens. The main constraint on supply is on the mining side: ore grades are falling, and new mines take more than a decade to permit and build.

## What to watch

Mine disruptions in Chile, Peru, Panama and Indonesia, treatment and refining charges, trade measures such as tariffs on refined copper, scrap exports from the EU, and grid investment plans.
