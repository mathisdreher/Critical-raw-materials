## What it is

Aluminium is refined in three steps. Bauxite ore is mined, mostly in open pits in the tropics. The Bayer process turns it into alumina, and electrolysis in smelters reduces alumina to metal. The EU list treats the three as one entry because a disruption at any step affects the others. Smelting is among the most electricity-intensive industrial processes, so the cost and carbon content of power largely determine where metal is made.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

Bauxite mining is led by Guinea, Australia and China, while China alone produces more than half of the world's primary aluminium. The EU imports most of its bauxite, with Guinea as the dominant origin, and has lost a significant part of its smelting capacity since the 2021 to 2022 energy crisis. Primary metal now comes largely from Norway, Iceland, Mozambique, the Gulf and India. Imports from Russia, historically a major supplier, have been progressively restricted by sanctions.

## Substitution and recycling

Aluminium is already heavily recycled: remelting scrap needs about 5% of the energy of primary production, and end-of-life scrap covers roughly a third of EU supply. The limit is scrap availability and alloy sorting rather than technology. Copper can replace aluminium in conductors and steel or composites in structures, generally at a weight or cost penalty.

## What to watch

European power prices and the fate of curtailed smelters, the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, which applies to aluminium, scrap export flows out of the EU, and Guinea's policy on bauxite exports and local refining.
