## What it is

Most vanadium is co-produced from vanadium-bearing titanomagnetite: the ore is smelted for iron, and vanadium is recovered from the steelmaking slag. Smaller amounts come from primary mines in South Africa and Brazil, and from oil residues, fly ash and spent catalysts. It is traded as vanadium pentoxide and as ferrovanadium.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

China produces about two thirds of the world's vanadium, followed by Russia, South Africa and Brazil. The EU has no mine output. Austria and Czechia convert imported oxides and slag into ferrovanadium. Russia was long the main supplier of vanadium to the EU, and its role has shrunk since 2022. Demand depends on Chinese rebar standards and construction activity, so prices are volatile: they tripled in 2018 and then fell back. Grid-scale flow batteries installed in China are becoming a visible second source of demand.

## Substitution and recycling

Niobium, titanium and manganese can replace vanadium in steel to varying degrees. Vanadium is recovered from spent catalysts and from industrial residues, and vanadium electrolyte can be reused indefinitely, which supports leasing models for flow batteries. Vanadium in steel is not recovered separately.

## What to watch

Chinese steel output and rebar standards, deployment of long-duration storage, recovery from slag and residues in Europe, and new primary projects in Australia.
