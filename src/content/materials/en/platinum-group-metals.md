## What it is

The six platinum group metals occur together and are mined together, mostly from the Bushveld Complex in South Africa, the Great Dyke in Zimbabwe, and as a by-product of nickel in Russia and North America. The mix of metals in each ore body is fixed, so the supply of minor metals such as iridium and ruthenium cannot respond to their own demand. The metals are quoted daily in US dollars per troy ounce.

## Supply chain and EU dependency

South Africa supplies about 70% of mined platinum and most rhodium, iridium and ruthenium. Russia is the largest palladium producer, on a par with South Africa. The EU has minor mine output in Finland and Poland but hosts some of the world's largest refiners and recyclers, in Belgium, Germany and Italy, with the United Kingdom next door. Electricity shortages and deep-mining costs in South Africa and the exposure to Russian palladium are the main risks. Palladium and rhodium prices fell steeply from their 2021 to 2022 peaks as electric vehicles reduced the outlook for autocatalysts.

## Substitution and recycling

Platinum and palladium substitute for each other in gasoline autocatalysts depending on price. Recycling is highly developed: spent autocatalysts, industrial catalysts and electronic scrap supply about a quarter of platinum and palladium each year. Iridium availability is a recognised constraint on PEM electrolyser deployment, and manufacturers are cutting loadings.

## What to watch

South African power supply and mine restructuring, any sanctions affecting Russian palladium, the speed of the shift to battery vehicles, and electrolyser orders.
